Before you start

Rules for every judge

  • Mark on your own. Do not discuss a student or look at another judge's marks until you have saved yours. The judge page never shows other judges' marks.
  • Step aside when you know someone. Friend, roommate, relative or same class group: tell your panel and do not mark that student. Another judge covers them.
  • No marks for branch, semester or connections. Every branch is welcome. Knowing a senior in the club earns nothing.
  • Judge thinking, not knowledge. 1st semester students have had only a few months of college. A good question asked out loud counts for more than a memorised answer.
  • Use the whole scale. A 3 is a normal, decent answer. Save 5 for answers that would make you want this person on your squad tomorrow.
  • Every student is a core candidate. Never mention supporting members, results or rankings to students. If they ask, say results come by WhatsApp and email by Saturday.
  • Be kind. For many of them this is their first interview. Smile, say your name, and thank every group.

Before the first group on each day, the judges in a panel agree out loud on what a 3 looks like for one sample answer. After the first two groups, compare totals quickly. If one judge is always 20 points higher or lower, talk about why.

One panel sees about 4 groups an hour, so about 16 to 20 students a day. If a squad has more than 20 students on one day, its seniors split into two panels with a teacher in each, or groups grow to 5. The admin checks the numbers on Tuesday evening and tells you which.

Teachers move between the panels and mark the groups they watch, so every student gets at least 2 marks sheets.

The criteria

How to mark

Each judge gives every student 1 to 5 on five things. The website multiplies each mark by its weight and adds them up, so the total is out of 100. A student's score is the average of all their judges' totals.

2Tried, but weak
3A normal, decent answer
4Better than most
5You want them on your squad

For every squad

Opening and commitment questions

Start every student with the opener. Use one commitment question per student somewhere in their 2 minutes.

  • Opener: Tell us your name, branch, and one thing you have made, fixed or organised that you are proud of.
  • The club meets on Saturdays and runs events like Bot Forge. Honestly, how many hours a week can you give?
  • Exams and a club event fall in the same week. What do you do?
  • Why RO-BIO, and why this squad?

Projects

G-07

Personal questions

  1. You have 4 weeks and Rs 2,000 to build something that helps a patient or a student. What would you build, and what would you do first?
  2. Tell us about something you tried that did not work. What did you do next?
  3. One person in your project team does nothing. What do you do?
  4. How would you split a line-following robot project between 4 people?
  5. Pick something on our website, like the robot arm or Robo Sumo. How would you improve it?
  6. What would you need to learn first to build a heart-rate monitor, and how would you learn it?
  7. How do you keep track of a project so nothing gets forgotten?

Group questions

  1. Choose one project the club should build this semester for under Rs 5,000. Agree on it, and on who does what.
  2. Design a robot that helps a person in a hospital. What does it do, and what is the simplest first version?
  3. The club has 3 project ideas and money for 1. How do you decide?
  4. Plan the first 2 weeks of building a Robo Sumo bot.

Events

G-09

Personal questions

  1. Bot Forge starts at 10. At 9:30 the projector in the hall does not work. What do you do?
  2. 150 students turn up for registration and there are 2 desks. How do you handle it?
  3. A judge arrives 40 minutes late. What changes in the schedule?
  4. Have you organised anything, like a school event, a fest or a family function? What went wrong, and how did you fix it?
  5. How would you get first year students to come to a Saturday workshop?
  6. Two teams argue about a Robo Sumo result and you are the volunteer nearby. What do you do?
  7. Out loud, make a checklist for the day before an event.

Group questions

  1. Plan a 1-hour Robo Sumo demo for first years. What happens, minute by minute?
  2. Bot Forge has 2 events and 1 hall. Plan the day.
  3. List the 5 things most likely to go wrong at Bot Forge, and who handles each.
  4. How do we make 100 participants feel welcome when they arrive?

Technical

G-07

Personal questions

  1. How do you think a line-following robot knows where the line is? There is no wrong answer. Think out loud.
  2. Your robot's motor runs on the bench but not on the floor. What could be wrong, and how would you check?
  3. What is something you took apart or fixed to see how it works?
  4. Arduino, a soldering iron, Python, C, a breadboard: which have you used? Which would you learn first?
  5. How would you explain what a sensor is to a school student?
  6. You get an error you have never seen before. Walk us through what you do.
  7. In Robo Sumo, how could a robot tell it is at the edge of the ring?

Group questions

  1. As a group, agree on the parts list for a simple line-following robot.
  2. Design a fair test to decide which of two Robo Sumo bots is stronger.
  3. How could something you wear tell that a person has fallen?
  4. Our robot keeps turning left. List every possible cause, then pick which to check first.

During the session

Using the judge page on your phone

  1. Open ro-bio.hub-muse.workers.dev/recruit/judge. The admin gives you the judge passcode in person.
  2. Type your name the same way both days, and pick your squad. Teachers pick "Teacher (all squads)".
  3. Pick today's day. Tick the 4 students in the group in front of you, then tap Mark this group.
  4. For each student, tap 1 to 5 on each criterion. Tap a criterion's name to see what 1, 3 and 5 mean. Add a note if you want, then tap Save.
  5. You can change your own marks until the admin locks marking. Students you have marked show a tick.

If the page will not load, write the marks on paper with the student's USN and enter them right after the session.

Thursday after 5 pm, or Friday

The selection meeting

  1. The admin opens the results page. Every squad's students are listed by average score, with each criterion's average and the judges' notes.
  2. Any student with fewer than 2 marks sheets is flagged. Get a second judge's marks before deciding on them.
  3. Each squad's seniors and a teacher pick 2 to 3 core members. Watch the "Core: x of 15" counter. It turns red above 15.
  4. From the next few students who showed real interest, pick the supporting members. Students are not told about this before results.
  5. Tie-break: higher squad skill average, then higher interest and commitment, then the squad head decides.
  6. If a strong student was not picked by their first choice squad, their second choice squad can take them, if it agrees.
  7. Lock marking, export the results, and send the result messages.
